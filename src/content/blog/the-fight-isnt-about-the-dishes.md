Nearly every couple I work with has a signature fight.

The dishes. The tone. Being late. How long it takes to answer a text. The details vary, but the shape is familiar. It has the same opening move, the same middle, and the same ending, and both people could usually perform the other person's lines from memory.

The fact that it keeps returning is important. A disagreement that is truly about dishes usually gets solved by a schedule. A disagreement that keeps coming back after the schedule was already working is usually about something else. Until that something else gets named, both people tend to keep concluding that the other one is not really trying.

## What is often underneath

The surface issue changes. What sits underneath it tends to be more consistent.

**Do I matter to you?** It rarely sounds that direct. It sounds like a complaint about the phone at dinner or about which family gets the weekend. But underneath it is often a question about priority and whether the relationship still holds real weight.

**Am I going to be left alone with this?** Arguments about chores and labor are often arguments about whether the partnership actually feels shared.

**Is it safe to need something from you?** Some people learned early that needing was dangerous, humiliating, or costly. In adulthood, the need often comes out sideways, through criticism, distance, or resentment.

**Am I about to be cast as the problem?** One partner may hear any complaint as a verdict on their character because that is how conflict worked in their family. They defend quickly. The defense lands as indifference. The complaint sharpens. The loop tightens.

That is the engine of many stuck arguments. Neither person is only responding to what is being said. Both are also responding to what it means, and those meanings were often assigned long before the relationship began.

## Why it repeats

Couples do not usually get stuck because they lack communication advice.

Many stuck couples are thoughtful, articulate, and already know the language. What keeps them trapped is that each person's move triggers the other's protective response. One reaches. The other withdraws to keep things from escalating. The withdrawal feels like abandonment, so the reaching gets more urgent. Then the withdrawal gets deeper.

Both moves made sense somewhere earlier in life. Together they form a loop that neither person would have built alone and that neither can fully interrupt by themselves.

That is why "we just need to communicate better" often falls flat. The words are arriving. The problem is that they are landing on older wounds and getting interpreted there.

## What couples work looks like

In couples therapy, we slow the fight down in real time.

That is the work more often than people expect. We take one recent example, often something small from the last week, and move through it slowly enough to notice what happened inside each of you at each turn. At full speed, that is almost impossible to see.

Usually there is a moment early in the sequence where something shifted before anything openly difficult was said. A tone landed a certain way. A pause got interpreted. That moment often matters more than resolving the surface topic, because it is where old material entered the room.

From there, we get curious about where each response came from. Not to assign blame. Not to explain anyone away. But understanding that your partner's shutdown is an old protection rather than a statement about your value changes what becomes possible next.

Over time, many couples stop having the fight in the same shape. The disagreement itself may still be real. What changes is that it stops carrying the weight of every previous version and becomes something the two of you can actually finish.

## Something worth knowing before you start

Couples therapy is not two individual therapies happening in the same room, and it does not require both people to be equally eager about coming.

A lot of good work begins with one partner who wanted therapy and another who agreed to come. It also does not require you to already know what is wrong. Many couples arrive with only the surface version and discover the real subject gradually.
