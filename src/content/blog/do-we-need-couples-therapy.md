Usually one of you has been wondering for a while.

Not because anything has broken, exactly. Things are mostly fine. But there's a conversation you've stopped having, or a subject that reliably goes sideways, or a distance that wasn't there a few years ago and neither of you can point to when it started. And somewhere in that is the question of whether this counts. Whether you're allowed to get help for something that hasn't become a crisis.

That question keeps a lot of couples waiting.

## The bar most people set

There's a common idea that couples therapy is for relationships in trouble. Affairs, constant fighting, someone with a foot out the door. By that standard, most couples never qualify until the situation has gotten much harder to work with.

The research on this is fairly consistent, and it's a little sobering: couples tend to wait years between noticing a problem and doing something about it. By then the pattern has had a long time to settle, and there's usually a layer of hurt on top of the original issue that has to be addressed before anyone can get to the original issue at all.

None of that means waiting was foolish. Most people wait because they're hoping it resolves on its own, which is reasonable, and because asking for help feels like an admission that something is wrong, which is a hard thing to say out loud about a relationship you care about.

## A more useful question

Instead of asking whether things are bad enough, it may help to ask whether anything has stopped moving.

Is there a subject the two of you route around. Does a particular argument arrive in the same shape every time, with the same ending. Have you found yourself editing what you say, not out of cruelty but out of fatigue, because you already know how it goes. Is there something you've decided to stop bringing up.

Those aren't emergencies. They're the early shape of distance, and they respond well to attention while they're still small.

Some couples come in with something specific: a decision they can't get through, a betrayal, a transition that's put more weight on the relationship than it was carrying before. Others come in because things are basically good and they'd like them to stay that way. Both are reasonable reasons to be in the room.

## Slowing it down

We take the thing that keeps happening and slow it down.

That's more of it than people expect. Usually we'll start with one recent example, often something small from the past week, and move through it slowly enough to see what happened inside each of you at every turn. At full speed that's invisible. Slowed down, there's almost always a moment early on where something shifted before anything difficult had actually been said.

From there we get curious about where each of your responses came from, and what they were originally for. Not to assign fault. Knowing that your partner's shutdown is an old protection rather than a verdict on you changes what's possible in the next conversation. [The fight isn't about the dishes](/blog/the-fight-isnt-about-the-dishes/) goes further into what tends to sit underneath a recurring argument.

## A few things worth knowing

It doesn't require you both to be equally enthusiastic. Plenty of good work starts with one partner who wanted this and one who agreed to come. That difference is usually worth talking about early, and it isn't a reason to stay home.

It also doesn't require you to know what's wrong. Most couples arrive with the surface version and find the real subject somewhere along the way.

And it isn't a referee service. I'm not there to determine who's right, which is sometimes disappointing in the first session and almost always a relief by the third.

One exception worth naming plainly: if there's fear in the relationship, or violence, or you find yourself working around your own safety, couples work isn't the right first step. Individual support is, and I'm glad to help you find it.

## If you've been sitting with this

You don't need permission, and you don't need the problem to be serious enough. Wanting the relationship to feel different is enough of a reason.
